exp-052 -- positive control for the RTRM cosine-similarity pronoun pictures
===========================================================================
Date: 2026-09-29. Model: GPT-2, layer 12. Pool: 30,099 L12 states (exp-050/051b pool).

QUESTION (Paul): not whether the model gets the pronoun right, but whether the
RTRM cosine-similarity method can VIEW the referent in the pronoun's neighborhood
when the pronoun is not confused. Do the spider/trophy pictures mean something,
or is the method blind even when resolution succeeds?

PREDICTED
---------
Behavioral: ~10/10 (the verb or the single noun disambiguates every item).
Geometry: the correct referent ranks <= 500 of 30,099 at the pronoun position
on all 10 items (6 verb-disambiguated pairs + 4 one-noun ceiling items).

Method: whole-question format ("...What does the word it/he refer to? Answer with
one word:"), next-token log-probability, two-alternative forced choice, recorded
the higher log-probability. Geometry: nearest-neighbor rank of each candidate's
closest pool occurrence at the pronoun state, identity excluded.

VERDICT
-------
The predeclared kill (referent > #500 on ALL FOUR ceiling items) did NOT trigger
as worded -- ceil1 shows dog at #110. But the substance of the kill landed:
3 of 4 ceiling items do NOT show the referent prominently, including cases where
the model demonstrably resolves the pronoun:
  - ceil2: behavior decisive for king (-4.36 vs -11.45), yet king is #17,061 --
    the referent is geometrically invisible.
  - ceil4: behavior correct for baby, yet the DISTRACTOR child is #96 while
    baby is #1,196.
  - ceil3: car #3,729 vs truck #3,896 -- referent barely ahead, neither prominent.

Two sharper findings:
1. Within each pair, the pronoun's neighborhood is IDENTICAL across the two
   readings (pair1a == pair1b, pair2a == pair2b, pair3a == pair3b). This is
   context-equivalence, not a discovery: the pronoun comes BEFORE the
   disambiguating verb ("because it was barking/meowing"), so its left context
   -- and hence its causal state -- is bit-identical. The verb-driven difference
   is invisible at the pronoun BY CONSTRUCTION. The pair design cannot test what
   it set out to test at the pronoun position. (My error in the design; the
   ceiling items carry the real test.)
2. Geometry and behavior do not track each other: pair1b geometry agrees with
   the WRONG answer (dog #139, model said dog); pair3b geometry favors car
   while the model (barely) said tree; ceil2/ceil4 geometry misses while the
   model is right and decisive.

INTERPRETATION (humble)
-----------------------
The RTRM cosine-similarity method is NOT blind: it can show the referent
prominently (dog #110/#139, man #87, boy #98). But absence of the referent in
the neighborhood does NOT mean absence of resolution -- the king case proves a
resolved pronoun can be geometrically invisible (#17,061 with decisive behavior).
Pronoun states live in pronoun-heavy neighborhoods (043b); the neighborhood
reflects the WORD (pronoun-ness), not the REFERENT, except sometimes.

Consequence for the public story: the spider/trophy pictures (referent far from
the pronoun) are consistent with non-resolution but cannot PROVE it -- the
pictures alone cannot carry "the model does not resolve the pronoun." That claim
rests on the behavioral legs (exp-047d full-vs-carrier, exp-049 association
account, exp-051 whole-question 28/54), which still stand. In the write-up the
pictures must be presented as illustration, not evidence; the numbers do the
claiming. exp-052 itself is the honest caveat and belongs in the record.
