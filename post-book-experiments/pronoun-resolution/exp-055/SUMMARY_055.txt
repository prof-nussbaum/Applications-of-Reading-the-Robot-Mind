SUMMARY exp-055: SME error-spotting on 10 NEW pronoun prompts
============================================================
Date: 2026-09-29. Paul's test: the SME uses ONLY the subtracted list.
What should they look for in new prompts to spot issues?

Method (Paul's framing): lossy compression via PCA. PCA finds the biggest
features that differentiate one token-state from another at the observation
layer (L12). We compress each state into 64 patterns, subtract the
reconstruction, and read the remainder. Query side additionally subtracts
the pronoun's mean state (054c-C2 recipe). torch.manual_seed(0) predeclared
and set before pca_lowrank.

Items: 14 candidates hand-written; stratified time-seeded draw (seed 1921 =
Jetson local HHMM, python random.Random): 5 clean + 5 tricky. Full audit at
AUDIT_055.txt. The 10 drawn prompts (verbatim):

CLEAN (referent = main-clause subject, competitor in relative clause):
 C1 "The man who met the boy was tired and he rested. What does the word he refer to? Answer with one word:" (label man)
 C4 "The cat that the dog chased was tired and it slept. What does the word it refer to? Answer with one word:" (label cat)
 C5 "The car that passed the truck was old and it rattled. What does the word it refer to? Answer with one word:" (label car)
 C6 "The truck that the car passed was old and it rattled. What does the word it refer to? Answer with one word:" (label truck)
 C7 "The man who saw the son was happy and he smiled. What does the word he refer to? Answer with one word:" (label man)
TRICKY (Winograd: disambiguating adjective AFTER the pronoun):
 T1 "The man couldn't lift the boy because he was weak. What does the word he refer to? Answer with one word:" (label man)
 T4 "The dog bit the cat because it was hurt. What does the word it refer to? Answer with one word:" (label cat)
 T5 "The car towed the truck because it was powerful. What does the word it refer to? Answer with one word:" (label car)
 T6 "The car towed the truck because it was broken. What does the word it refer to? Answer with one word:" (label truck)
 T7 "The man praised the son because he was proud. What does the word he refer to? Answer with one word:" (label man)

SME indicators (predeclared, applied mechanically):
 I1 referent presence: label rank <= 100 in subtracted list?
 I2 competitor check: other sentence noun ranked BELOW the label?
 clean verdict: CORRECT iff I1 and I2 else RISK
 tricky verdict: lean = better-ranked noun; LEAN-OK iff lean == label else LEAN-MISMATCH
 I4 cross-check: model's own head at the pronoun, 053 recipe (RAW hs[12] @ W.T;
   note: run_055.py's pron_logit fields used the normalized state by mistake
   and read ~0 margins; corrected in run_055b.py / results_055b_pronlogits.json,
   KING sanity check reproduces 053's +5.64 exactly).
Behavior: next-token forced choice ' n1' vs ' n2' after 'Answer with one word:'.

 tricky: lean == first noun >=4/5; mismatch overturned correctly >=60%.

What the experiment says the SME should look for (refined checklist):
 1. Is the intended referent in the subtracted list (top-100)? C7 (man #188)
    and T7 (man #5,469): absent -> the state doesn't carry the answer, the
    model guessed (and guessed wrong). Red flag.
 2. Is any other sentence noun ranked above it? C6 (car #7 over truck #12),
    T6 (car #17 over truck #22): competitor live -> model followed it and
    erred. But C1/C4 show this flag also fires when the model still gets it
    right (as baby/child did). Yellow flag: contested, check the scorer.
 3. (Tricky) Which noun does the pronoun lean toward vs what the sentence
    requires? A wrong lean means the rest of the sentence must overturn it
    downstream - watch the final answer closely.
 4. What does the model's own head read at the pronoun? On C1, C4, T4 the
    head favored the TRUE referent where the cosine list leaned to the
    competitor - 053's "head reads what cosine obscures," replicated on new
    items. The list shows candidates; the head reads the fine print.
 5. The scorer has the last word. C7: head at pronoun leaned man (0.29) but
    the final answer was son - downstream processing moved the decision.
    Flags are sensitive (4/4 errors caught) but not specific (3 false alarms).

Operational answer to Paul's "mushy" question: the SME uses the SUBTRACTED
list only. The raw list was scaffolding to motivate subtraction, not a second
instrument - the raw-vs-subtracted difference is the same signal buried vs
revealed, not the signal itself. Retire it from the operational story.

Qualifications (full prominence):
- n=10 pilot; thin neighborhoods: cat (8 pool occurrences), truck (6).
- Near-tie margins are noise: T4 (0.01), C7 (0.33), T7 (0.21). Robust errors:
  T6 (1.83 nats, decisive), C6 (0.72).
- T7's label is the softest of the 10 ("praised the son because he was
  proud" - the proud one is the praiser, but the 0.21-nat near-tie shows the
  model found it genuinely ambiguous).
- The first-noun lean is a tendency, not a law: C1/C4 leaned to the RECENT
  noun (boy/dog), T7 leaned son. Recency competes with subjecthood.
- results_055.json's pron_logit_lean fields are SUPERSEDED by
  results_055b_pronlogits.json (normalization bug in the I4 cross-check;
  RTRM and behavioral columns unaffected).
