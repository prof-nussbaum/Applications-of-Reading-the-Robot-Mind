exp-054: PCA-as-codec residual test (Paul's Kaggle trick on hidden states)
=========================================================================
Date: 2026-09-29. Machine: Jetson Orin Nano. No GPU training; PCA on CPU.

IDEA (Paul's): use an autoencoder AS A CODEC -- subtract its reconstruction
from the original state vector. The residual keeps what the AE could NOT
capture (the small perturbations) with the big carrier variations removed.
Then run the cosine neighbor search on RESIDUALS. This is his Kaggle
power-line trick (AE learns 'normal', the residual reveals the anomaly)
turned inward on the model.

SUBSTITUTION DISCLOSED: Paul's pretrained AE weights are not on either
machine (searched Jetson + workspace thoroughly 2026-09-29; only the book's
decoder-to-tokens code exists, which outputs vocab logits and cannot serve
as a vector codec). This run uses the no-training equivalent: a LINEAR
autoencoder with a k-dim neck IS PCA (k=64). Codec(h) = mu + V V^T (h - mu),
fit on the 30,099-state search pool only; test states projected with the
pool's PCs, no refit. Rerun with his weights the moment he produces them.

PREDICTED (before running): if decision-relevant info lives in small
perturbations drowned by big carrier variations, removing the top-64 PCs
lets the referent surface -- ALL FOUR ceiling referents rank <= 500 by
cosine in residual space (exp-052's bar). Raw ranks: dog #110, king #17,061,
car #3,729, baby #1,196. Raw ranks recomputed here as pipeline validation
and reproduced exp-052 exactly.

ACTUAL:
- Bar PASSED: residual ranks -- dog #125, king #127, car #45, baby #493.
  All four <= 500. The drowned surfaced: king 17,061 -> 127, car 3,729 -> 45,
  tree 14,153 -> 1,697, cat 16,360 -> 73.
- The top-64 PCs capture 99.16% of pool variance; the residual is 0.84% of
  the state's energy. Paul's 'small perturbations' are literally < 1% of the
  vector -- and the referent is in there.
- QUALIFICATION (full prominence): the residual does NOT adjudicate correct
  vs wrong by itself. The WRONG candidates surfaced too: farmer 13,244 -> 192
  (vs king 127), truck 3,896 -> 68 (vs car 45), cat 16,686 -> 160 (vs dog
  125), child 96 -> 99 (vs baby 493 -- distractor still wins big), car 81
  (vs tree 1,697 on pair3b -- distractor wins big). The residual reveals
  drowned NOUN content generally, not the winner. Picking the winner is the
  head's job (king wins behaviorally by 7.09 nats).
- The already-visible referents got slightly WORSE (dog 110 -> 125,
  man 87 -> 114, boy 98 -> 124): the top-64 carried some legitimate shared
  content too, not only the drowning carrier. The residual is a different
  lens, not a strictly better space.
- Residual neighborhoods are still sane (top-5 neighbors are pronouns:
  'it'/'he'/'she'), not noise -- syntactic-slot structure survives even in
  the sub-1% energy band.

REFINED, NOT REFUTED: Paul's drowning hypothesis is confirmed mechanically
-- remove the big variations and the referent appears. But 'the residual
reveals the answer' is too strong; it reveals the candidates. One cascade
level was enough; per his Kaggle experience (deeper = more bits needed),
a second level would be compressing near-noise.

CAVEATS: linear codec only -- his nonlinear AE (trained on its crummy data)
might capture more carrier and give a cleaner residual. Pool states were
unit-normalized as saved; PCA ran in that space (the same space searched).
No new dataset; test items are the audited exp-052 ten.

NEXT: rerun with Paul's actual AE weights when located; or test whether the
residual correct-vs-wrong gap tracks behavioral margins over a larger item
set (here: 6/10 sign agreement, disagreements on the hard items).
