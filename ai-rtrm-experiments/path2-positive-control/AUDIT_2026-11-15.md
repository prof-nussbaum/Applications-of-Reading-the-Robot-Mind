## Construction and draw

- Template families per the protocol: 60 number-agreement + 60
  gender-agreement items. Candidates generated programmatically from
  8 number frames x 20 noun pairs and 9 gender frames x 24 pairs
  (20 first-name pairs, 4 role pairs), each frame in both pronoun
  variants with referent-fitting predicates: 2,100 distinct candidate
  sentences (number 1,020; gender 1,080).
- Draw: seed = local time HHMM at run moment = 0910 (2026-11-15
  09:10:01 EST, Jetson local time). PRNG: Python random.Random(910);
  each family list shuffled with that instance; final items = first 60
  of each shuffled family list. Full shuffled order is preserved in
  draw_record_control.json, so any replacement comes from the next
  position of the same list.
- Final set: items_control.jsonl, 120 items (number 60, gender 60),
  same JSONL shape as confirmatory_items.jsonl plus family/label/
  competitor fields. Pronoun split: number it 37 / they 23; gender
  he 36 / she 24 (the seeded draw was not hand-balanced; recorded as a
  known asymmetry). Label position: number label_index 0/1 = 27/33;
  gender 29/31.

## Check 1 — exact prompt text, well-formed

Prompt format is the Path 2 question format, verbatim:
"<sentence> What does the word <pronoun> refer to? Answer with one word:"
Six verbatim prompts from the drawn set (3 per family):

1. "The horses chased the cow because they were fast. What does the
   word they refer to? Answer with one word:" — label: horses.
2. "The pigs followed the spider because it was quiet. What does the
   word it refer to? Answer with one word:" — label: spider.
3. "The mouse chased the pigs because they were loud. What does the
   word they refer to? Answer with one word:" — label: pigs. (Scene is
   strained; agreement is still unique. Kept deliberately: a control
   item may be implausible as a scene as long as grammar settles it.)
4. "Carl met Sarah at the school because he was late. What does the
   word he refer to? Answer with one word:" — label: Carl.
5. "The waiter thanked the waitress because she had been kind. What
   does the word she refer to? Answer with one word:" — label: waitress.
6. "Anna waited for George because she was patient. What does the word
   she refer to? Answer with one word:" — label: Anna.

REJECTED before the draw (eligibility, see Check 4), verbatim:
"The king thanked the queen because she had been kind." — queen has 3
built-pool token-label occurrences, below the >=10 floor. The pair
king/queen was excluded at word selection, so no king/queen item
entered the candidate pool.
Design-stage rejections, also recorded: number-ambiguous nouns (sheep,
deer, fish) and collective nouns (team, group) were excluded from the
word lists entirely — a "they/it" item built on them would not be
settled by agreement. Predicates embedding a second gendered pronoun
(e.g. "because he missed her") were excluded from the gender frames:
the extra pronoun is a confound the scored pronoun does not need.

## Check 2 — label defensibility

Every one of the 120 items was read by hand after the draw.
- Number: the because-clause verb (was/were) agrees with exactly one
  candidate. Plural candidate + "they were" or singular candidate +
  "it was"; no item admits the other reading grammatically. World
  knowledge never has to be consulted, and where a scene is implausible
  (mouse chasing pigs) it does not point against the agreement reading.
- Gender: candidates are unambiguously gendered (first names with a
  single conventional gender; role pairs father/mother, son/daughter,
  waiter/waitress, boy/girl). Because-clause predicates are
  gender-neutral by construction (early, late, worried, had news,
  needed help, was ill, was kind...), so plausibility does not settle
  any item in either direction; the pronoun gender does.
- No item was found whose label a careful reader would dispute.
  Replacements needed: NONE.

## Check 3 — vacuous-by-construction risks

- Candidate frequency asymmetry: pool counts of the two heads within
  an item differ by at most a factor of 3.0 (worst: John 30 / Sarah 10,
  items 71 and 95). No item exceeds 3x; none is near the 5x flag line.
- No candidate is absent or rare as a pool token label: minimum head
  count over all 240 candidate slots is exactly 10 (the floor).
- Pronouns themselves are common pool labels (they 200, it 204,
  he 214, she 204 occurrences), so pronoun states are not thin.
- Every head word is a single GPT-2 token in " <word>" context
  (checked with the tokenizer for all candidates; a split token would
  leave no whole-word pool state to rank).
- "it" is referential in every number item (no expletive/weather
  frames); "they" is never used for a singular candidate.

## Expected behaviour (for the gate, to be tested in the scoring pass)

Agreement here is local and grammatical: the disambiguating verb or
pronoun gender sits inside the same clause as the pronoun, and both
candidates are present as ordinary nouns/names. GPT-2 handles
subject-verb number agreement and gendered-pronoun resolution with
named referents far above its DPR Winograd-style accuracy in prior
battery work. Prediction (locked with the protocol): forced-choice
accuracy >= 80%, so the gate passes and the checklist test on these
items is interpretable either way. If the gate fails, the protocol
says the control has failed as a control and no checklist conclusion
is drawn.
