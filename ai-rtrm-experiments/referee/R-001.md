## 1. Main finding: 474 of the 617 items are twin pairs with identical input to the instrument

DPR items come in pairs: the same sentence up to and including the pronoun,
the same two candidates, a different clue after the pronoun, and the opposite
label. In the confirmatory set:

- **237 twin pairs = 474 of 617 items (77%).** 467 of those items are class B.
- In **all 237 pairs** the saved list ranks are identical with label and
  competitor swapped. GPT-2 is causal, so the pronoun state is the same vector
  for both twins.

Consequences that follow by construction, not by measurement:

1. **Lean-versus-label is fixed at exactly 50% on twins.** Whatever the list
   leans toward is the label for one twin and the competitor for the other.
   Observed overall: 312/617 = 50.6%.
2. **Flag leg (a) fires on exactly one twin of every pair.** It cannot
   discriminate errors from correct answers on twins unless the model's pick
   happens to line up with the lean.
3. **Correctness is not identifiable from the pronoun state on twins.** The
   state does not contain the label. The only thing a pronoun-state readout
   can possibly predict there is *which noun the model will pick*.

The paper already says the class-B flag "can only ever be a pre-clue
structural-prior warning". That is true but understates it: for three quarters
of the test bed, the locked target (was the model right?) was undecidable from
the instrument's input.

## 2. The ceiling probe's AUC of 0.316 is twin leakage, not overfitting

`RESULTS_CONTROL.md` reads the probe's test AUC of 0.316 (CI 0.262 to 0.377,
train 0.914) as "the signature of an overfit probe" and concludes that
"Study 1's null is a statement about the state".

An overfit probe lands near 0.5 on held-out data. A confidence interval
entirely below 0.5 means the probe is systematically wrong, and the twins
explain it:

- The split was by item, so twins were separated into train and test.
- Twins have identical features.
- In **217 of 237 pairs (91.6%)** the model picked the same noun for both
  twins in the question format, so `model_right` is opposite within the pair.

A probe that memorises the training twin therefore gets the test twin wrong.
The 0.316 is a property of the split.

**Requested change:** withdraw the "statement about the state" reading. If the
probe is kept, rerun it with the split grouped by twin pair, and separately
with the model's *pick* (head1 or head2) as the target.

## 3. What the list does track: weakly, the model's pick

Using the substitution pick as the model's decision:

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Lean = label | 312/617 = 50.6% (forced on twins) |
| Lean = question-format pick | 325/617 = 52.7% |
| Lean = substitution pick, all items | 354/617 = 57.4% (CI 53.5 to 61.3) |
| Lean = substitution pick, non-twin items | 90/143 = 62.9% (CI 55.0 to 70.9) |
| Twin pairs where the model picked the same noun twice: lean = that noun | 105/183 = 57.4%, pair-level z = 2.00 |

Not a shared position bias alone: P(lean = first noun) is 51.4% when the model
picks the first noun and 39.2% when it picks the second.

So the 0.579 balanced accuracy against substitution-defined errors is the lean
weakly agreeing with the model's pick. It is a signal about the **decision**,
and by section 1 it cannot be a signal about the correct referent.

**The reported Fisher p = 4.6e-6 should be withdrawn or replaced.** It treats
617 items as independent. Twin items share one state and have mirrored flags
and outcomes. The pair-level test gives 105 of 183, roughly p = 0.02 one-sided.

Flag versus substitution-defined error, split by twin status:

| Items | n | Catch | False alarm | Balanced accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | 617 | 84.0% | 68.1% | 0.579 |
| Twins | 474 | 84.0% | 70.4% | 0.568 |
| Non-twins | 143 | 84.1% | 60.0% | 0.621 |

Also useful for the paper: in 54 twin pairs the substitution pick changes with
the clue, and the model is right on both twins in 52 of them. In the other 183
pairs it picks the same noun whatever the clue says. That is the cleanest
description of GPT-2 small on this set.

## 4. Paul's three questions

### Q1. Is "these readouts recover what a model has, not what it will do" stated at the strength the evidence supports?

No. The "has" half is supported. The "not what it will do" half is not, for
three reasons.

- **The number-control items cannot separate the two.** The model is 100%
  correct by substitution, so the label *is* the decision. The README reads the
  lens and list results there as "availability, not decisions"; the data cannot
  tell which.
- **Where they can be separated, the evidence points the other way.** On DPR
  the list carries no information about the label (it cannot) and weak
  information about the pick (section 3).
- **The lens at hs[11] recovers the label, and so the decision, on 86.7% of
  number items**, against 56.7% at hs[12] and 65.0% for the list, which reads
  hs[12]. One layer changes the answer by 30 points. A claim about what
  "these readouts" cannot do needs a layer sweep behind it.

The genuine has-without-does evidence in the record is narrower: the 14
STORED-but-failed forward items in exp-201, and the set-wide familiarity lift
in the reversed probes.

Wording the evidence supports:

> These readouts reliably recover content: the words of the sentence,
> grammatical number, and which tokens the model has been exposed to. Their
> relation to the model's decision was weak in the one place we could measure
> it, depended sharply on the layer read, and was untestable on most of
> Study 1's items, where the correct answer was not in the state that was read.

### Q2. Is "too weak to use, and invisible against format-defined errors" the fair statement for Study 1?

Half fair. "Too weak to use" stands: the flag fires on two thirds of correct
items under either definition of error. "Invisible against format-defined
errors" is true but gives the wrong cause. The larger cause is section 1.
Suggested statement:

> On 77% of items the correct answer could not be read from the pronoun state
> by any method, because twin items share that state and have opposite labels.
> What the list weakly tracks is the model's pick (57% agreement, pair-level
> p about 0.02). As an error warning it is too weak to use.

The locked NOT WIN verdict is untouched by any of this.

### Q3. The one experiment that would most strengthen or kill the claim

**A twin-pair pick-tracking test at the answer position, across layers.**

- **Items:** the 237 twin pairs already in hand.
- **Read position:** the final prompt token of the question format, where the
  model actually makes its choice and where the clue is in view. Not the
  pronoun.
- **Target:** the model's own pick in that same format. Predicting the pick
  rather than correctness removes both the format-noise problem and the twin
  problem.
- **Readouts:** the cosine neighbour list and the logit lens, at each of
  layers 6 to 12.
- **Scoring, at the pair level:** in the pairs where the model's pick flips
  between twins, does the readout's lean flip with it? In the pairs where the
  pick stays put, does the lean stay put?
- **What it settles:** if a readout's lean follows the pick well above chance
  at some layer, "not what it will do" is dead for that readout and the paper
  has a positive result. If no layer follows the pick even at the answer
  position, the claim is earned and can be stated plainly.

It needs no retraining. The pick-flipping pairs in the question format are few
(about 20), so the protocol should fix a minimum count in advance and add the
non-twin items as a second stratum. Bars should be locked before any
answer-position state is extracted.

## 5. Smaller points

- **Substitution with proper names.** The method writes "the" + candidate, so
  name items are scored as "the Bob" against "the Bill". Both sides carry the
  same oddity, but report substitution accuracy separately for name and
  common-noun items.
- **Positive control, lean-match.** The lean-match rates (46.7% overall, 31.2%
  on errors) are against the question-format pick, the format that failed the
  gate. Add lean against the substitution pick for the same 120 items.
- **Token-exposure protocol.** No change requested. One suggestion for the
  write-up: report specificity for the 30 reverse-trained pairs and their 30
  forward-only partners side by side per seed, since that contrast is the
  result.
- **Robot work.** The README's framing cites the robot results as a second
  modality. The faithful re-run of exp-320 was scored against the wrong prompt
  table in 9 of 21 pairs (see `rescore_faithful.py`, sent to Paul on
  2026-11-12). Until that rescore is run, the robot half should not carry
  weight in this paper.
