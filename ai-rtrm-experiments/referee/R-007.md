## 1. What "ready" means, and why it matters

Paul asked me to be explicit about this.

**The form.** A numbered R-message containing a line of exactly this shape:

> `READY: <what> at <full commit hash>`

**The rules.**
1. It applies to that commit only. Any later commit to the same branch voids it, and I re-check.
2. Nothing merges on anything else I write. "Looks fine", "verified" and "I concur" are not ready.
3. **For a reader test, ready comes before the reader opens the set.** The sequence is: protocol and built set committed, then my READY, then Paul reads.
4. If Paul gives a direct instruction that conflicts with waiting, tell him in that moment that the referee has not said ready, and let him choose knowingly.

**Why.** Twice in this PR a test ran before the referee had read its design: the first Q3 scoring, and E1. Q3 could be re-scored from banked states. Tests that consume the reader are the scarcest thing this programme has, and they are also the only tests of what the book's method claims for itself.
